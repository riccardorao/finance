Il Dividendo di Firenze

A working model of the proposal: a progressive tourist tax that falls hardest on mass-market and luxury stays, frees under-30 EU visitors, nudges short lets back to residents, and returns the surplus to the people who live here. Move the levers on the left; every figure on the page recalculates.

2 · Behavioural response

Overnight stays by visitor type

Each persona's nightly price follows a log-normal spread around its median. Demand reacts to the all-in nightly cost with a constant elasticity; hosts absorb part of any increase. Part of the lost stays moves to the rail- and tram-linked metropolitan belt and comes back by day.

3 · Integrated municipal account

From tax take to structural balance

Today's €82m already pays for existing services, so the dividend can only come from the increase. Balance = (net extra tourist tax − collateral losses + operating savings) − Fondo Armonizzatore.

4 · Housing market

Short lets back to residents

Each short-let flat earns some multiple R of what it would earn on a long let. Owners switch when the reform cuts their short-let income below what a long let pays after a risk discount. The rent guarantee fund shrinks that discount and so moves the switch point.

Sources and method

Where the numbers come from

    Calibration. The model rescales current tariffs × stays × taxable share so that the baseline reproduces the 2025 revenue estimate. The same coefficient (it absorbs the 7-night cap, other exemptions and compliance) is applied to the new tariffs.

    Recorded vs actual stays. Official statistics count about 9.2m nights but undercount short lets; revenue of €77–82m at €3.50–8 a night implies roughly 13–14m taxed nights. The default of 14m includes unrecorded short lets.

    Limits. Partial-equilibrium, one city, annual steady state. It does not model hotel supply responses, competition with other Italian art cities, exchange rates, or the general-equilibrium effect of the dividend on local spending.

    Model built for discussion. Figures are estimates under the stated assumptions, not forecasts by the Comune di Firenze.
